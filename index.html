Part 1The words: 21 dated terms, by documented first appearance

21 words for a changing climate

Each mark is a documented milestone, not necessarily an invention. Squares are dates the source flags as prominence or not-first-use. Ten further terms (e.g. climate grief, net zero) are left out because no origin date is established for them. Click a term. Category colours are my own grouping.

Select a term to read its meaning, origin and caveat.

Many of those words name feelings: anxiety, grief, shame. Researchers have started to measure them.

Part 2The feeling: youth survey, 10 countries

What young people carry

The worry pit

Pick a worry to drop it into the pit, and hold to keep pouring. Each ball's size follows its percentage, deliberately exaggerated so the big worries stand out (diameter grows faster than the percentage, so sizes are not area-proportional), and tapping a ball tells you what it is. Balls are statements, not people, and the measures overlap, so the pile is a feeling of weight, not a total.

Part 2Yale Program on Climate Change Communication, US adults, Dec 2022

Who feels it most?

Percent of US adults scoring at or above the screening threshold on climate-specific questions, by group. Group samples are small (109 to 776 people), with margins of error of roughly 3 to 9 points, so small differences may be noise. These screens are not diagnoses.

How to read: each bar is the share of that group scoring at or above the threshold. The dark bar is all US adults, for comparison. Small gaps between groups may be noise, because the group samples are small.

LimitsRead before concluding
Most numbers come from US adults (Yale) and a ten-country youth sample (Lancet), so they should not be read as a world picture. Subgroup samples are small (n = 109 to 776). Rounding is independent, and a 0% does not prove no cases. Vocabulary years come from dictionary records and secondary sources and are earliest-known, not coinage dates.
